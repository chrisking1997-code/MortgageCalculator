> Written in November 2026. Figures are based on Freddie Mac's Primary Mortgage Market Survey for the week ending 12 November 2026. Actual offers depend on your credit, down payment and lender.

According to Freddie Mac, the average **30-year fixed mortgage rate was 7.11%** in mid-November 2026, with the **15-year fixed at 6.45%**. Rates had fallen for four straight weeks but were still well above the 6.24% average of a year earlier.

For anyone buying a home or deciding how to pay down a loan, the numbers below show how much the choice of term and repayment strategy matters.

## 30-year vs 15-year on a $400,000 loan

| | 30-year fixed at 7.11% | 15-year fixed at 6.45% |
|---|---|---|
| Monthly principal and interest | about $2,691 | about $3,473 |
| Total interest over the loan | about $569,000 | about $225,000 |

The 15-year loan costs about **$780 more per month**, but saves roughly **$344,000 in interest**. The 30-year loan's advantage is flexibility: a lower required payment, with the option to pay more when you can.

Compared with a year ago, when rates averaged 6.24%, the same 30-year loan would cost about **$231 more per month** today.

## What extra payments save

On the 30-year loan above, paying an extra **$300 a month** toward principal:

- Pays the loan off in about **267 months** (around 22 years instead of 30)
- Saves roughly **$173,000** in interest

At higher rates, every extra dollar of principal saves more interest, which is why extra payments become more attractive when rates are above 7%. Before overpaying, check that you have an emergency fund, no higher-interest debt, and no prepayment penalty on your loan.

## Refinancing later

One feature of the US system is the option to **refinance** if rates fall. For example, if 30-year rates dropped to 6%, a new $400,000 loan would cost about $2,398 a month. Refinancing has closing costs, so the usual test is how many months of savings it takes to cover them. We explain the break-even approach in [Is refinancing worth it?](/blog/mortgage-refinance/) (Chinese).

## How this differs from Taiwan

- **US:** most borrowers lock a single rate for 15 or 30 years. Rate rises hurt new buyers, but existing borrowers with fixed loans are protected.
- **Taiwan:** most mortgages float with the bank's base rate. Rates are much lower (around 2% in recent years), but every central bank move feeds through to existing borrowers.
- **UK:** borrowers usually fix for 2 to 5 years and then refix, so rate changes arrive in steps. See our [UK base rate article](/blog/en-uk-base-rate-remortgage/).
