> Written in November 2026. Central bank decisions are announced on their own schedules; nothing here is a forecast.

After several years of cuts or pauses, interest rates are moving up again in major economies:

- **United States:** the Federal Reserve raised its target range in September 2026 to 3.75%–4.00%, and futures markets were pricing a high chance of another rise at its December meeting.
- **United Kingdom:** the Bank of England raised Bank Rate to 4% on 5 November 2026, citing inflation of 3.5%.
- **Taiwan:** with inflation forecast above 2%, several banks and some central bank board members have said a December rise of half a quarter-point (0.125 percentage points) cannot be ruled out.

Taiwan does not have to follow the US or UK, but global rates affect Taiwanese households through several channels.

## 1. Mortgages

Most Taiwanese mortgages are floating-rate, so any move by the central bank feeds through to existing borrowers. A 0.125 percentage point rise adds about **NT$638 a month** to a NT$10 million, 30-year loan at 2.2%; a cumulative 1 percentage point rise adds about NT$5,277. Details are in our Chinese article on the [possible December rate rise](/blog/rate-hike-mortgage-impact/).

## 2. Deposits

Higher rates are good news for savers. Time deposit rates tend to rise after a central bank increase, which reduces the cost of keeping your emergency fund in cash. But check whether deposit rates are still below inflation; if they are, cash is still losing purchasing power.

## 3. The exchange rate

When US rates are much higher than Taiwan's, money tends to flow toward US dollar assets, which can put pressure on the New Taiwan dollar. Exchange rates are driven by many factors and are hard to predict, but a few practical points hold:

- If you will **spend** US dollars (study, travel, US investments), building up USD gradually reduces timing risk.
- If you are holding USD deposits **only for the interest**, remember that currency moves can wipe out the rate advantage. Our example shows a 4% USD deposit at 32 TWD/USD breaks even at about 30.8 after a year. See [USD deposits and FX risk](/blog/usd-deposit-fx-risk/) (Chinese).

## 4. Investments

Rising rates usually raise the yield on new bonds and can weigh on share prices, especially for companies valued on distant future profits. That does not mean anyone can time the market. For long-term investors, a rising-rate period is a good moment to check that your allocation still matches your risk tolerance, rather than to make big bets. See [asset allocation basics](/blog/asset-allocation-basics/) (Chinese).

## What households can do now

1. **Stress-test your mortgage** at 0.5 and 1 percentage point higher using our [mortgage calculator](/mortgage).
2. **Review where your cash sits.** Make sure your emergency fund earns a reasonable rate.
3. **Separate currency needs from interest chasing.** Hold foreign currency for planned spending, not just yield.
4. **Avoid adding variable-rate debt** you could not comfortably carry at higher rates.
