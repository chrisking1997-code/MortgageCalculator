Recently, I conducted "financial diagnoses" for several highly-paid executives in tech and foreign firms, and I noticed a terrifying commonality.

They are all aggressively planning for FIRE (Financial Independence, Retire Early). The target numbers they fill out usually land between 25M and 30M TWD. Their current monthly expenses are about 80K TWD. Working backwards using the traditional "4% Rule," saving up 24M TWD seems like it would guarantee a safe retirement.

I usually ask them just one question: **"How many years from now do you plan to retire? 10 years? 15 years? Are you sure that 80K then will still afford you the exact same quality of life you have today?"**

Most people freeze. This is the biggest blind spot when pursuing FIRE: **We are too accustomed to defining "future financial freedom" using "current purchasing power."**

### The Underestimated Invisible Tax: Inflation's "Negative Compounding"

Everyone loves calculating how much money a 7% annualized ETF return can compound into, but they deliberately ignore the 2.5% to 3% "negative compounding" of inflation.

Let's do the cruel math. Assume you are 35 years old and plan to retire at 50 (15 years from now), currently requiring 80K TWD a month to maintain your lifestyle.

If you use *that* future real expense to reverse-engineer the 4% Rule, your safe retirement baseline isn't 24M TWD—it's **34.5M TWD**.

That massive 10M gap is the fatal reason why many early retirees are forced to go back to work at 55.

### Stop Investing on Feeling, Let the Data Speak

Your asset allocation cannot be designed solely for "asset growth"; it must possess the defensive power of being "inflation-resistant" and "cycle-proof." Whether it's pouring money into broad-market ETFs, real estate arbitrage, or allocating into highly physical assets like whole whisky casks, the core logic remains the same: **Your asset growth rate must ruthlessly crush the inflation growth rate.**
