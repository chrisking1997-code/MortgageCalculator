People often share their FIRE (Financial Independence, Retire Early) targets with me. A typical case: current monthly expenses of about 80K TWD. Working backwards using the traditional "4% Rule," saving up 24M TWD seems like it would guarantee a safe retirement.

I usually ask them just one question: **"How many years from now do you plan to retire? 10 years? 15 years? Are you sure that 80K then will still afford you the exact same quality of life you have today?"**

Most people freeze. This is the biggest blind spot when pursuing FIRE: **We are too accustomed to defining "future financial freedom" using "current purchasing power."**

### The Underestimated Invisible Tax: Inflation's "Negative Compounding"

Everyone loves calculating how much money a 7% annualized ETF return can compound into, but often ignore the 2.5% to 3% "negative compounding" of inflation.

Let's do the cruel math. Assume you are 35 years old and plan to retire at 50 (15 years from now), currently requiring 80K TWD a month to maintain your lifestyle.

In 15 years, just to maintain that exact same "80K lifestyle," you will need about **116K** every month.
If you use *that* future real expense to reverse-engineer the 4% Rule, your safe retirement baseline isn't 24M TWD—it's about **34.8M TWD**.

That gap of more than 10M TWD is one reason some early retirees find their money running short and return to work.

### Stop Investing on Feeling, Let the Data Speak

Whatever you invest in, the core requirement is the same: **over the long run, your assets need to grow faster than inflation.** A simple way to plan is to use a *real* return (nominal return minus inflation) when you project your savings, so the result is expressed in today's purchasing power.
