Our [mortgage calculator](/mortgage) supports three countries because their mortgage systems are built on genuinely different ideas. Understanding the differences helps whether you are buying abroad, comparing offers, or simply reading the news about interest rates.

## The short version

| | Taiwan | United States | United Kingdom |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical rate type | Floating, linked to bank base rate | Fixed for the whole term | Fixed for 2–5 years, then refix |
| Typical term | 20–40 years | 15 or 30 years | 25–35 years |
| Interest-only option | Grace period, usually 1–5 years | Uncommon | Available but less common |
| Who carries rate risk | Borrower, continuously | Lender (borrower can refinance if rates fall) | Borrower, every time a fix ends |
| Rate level (late 2026) | Around 2%+ | Around 7% for 30-year fixed | Around 5–6% for new fixes |

The rate levels above are approximate and change constantly.

## Taiwan: low floating rates, plus grace periods

Most Taiwanese mortgages float with the lender's base rate, which moves with central bank policy. Rates have been low by international standards, around 2% in recent years. A distinctive feature is the **grace period**: for the first one to five years you can pay interest only.

Example: NT$10 million over 30 years at 2.2% costs about **NT$37,970 a month**. With a three-year grace period, the first three years cost about NT$18,333 a month, then the payment rises to about NT$40,960 because the full principal must be repaid over 27 years.

The risk: every rate change affects every borrower, and grace periods can create a payment jump. See [Grace periods: poison or cure?](/blog/en-grace-period-arbitrage/).

## United States: lock it in for 30 years

The 30-year fixed mortgage is the standard in the US. Your rate never changes, which makes budgeting simple and protects you when rates rise. If rates fall, you can refinance, although that has closing costs.

Example: $400,000 over 30 years at 7.11% costs about **$2,691 a month**. Paying $300 extra a month clears it in about 22 years and saves about $173,000 in interest. More in [US mortgage rates above 7%](/blog/en-us-30-year-fixed-mortgage/).

The risk: high rates make homes less affordable for new buyers, and many existing owners are reluctant to move because they would lose a lower locked rate.

## United Kingdom: fix, then refix

UK borrowers usually take a two- or five-year fixed deal. When it ends, they either arrange a new deal or roll onto the lender's standard variable rate (SVR), which is usually much higher.

Example: £250,000 over 25 years on a two-year fix at 4.5% costs about **£1,390 a month**. If the borrower then rolls onto a 7.5% SVR, the payment rises to about £1,817. More in [Bank of England raises rates to 4%](/blog/en-uk-base-rate-remortgage/).

The risk: payments can jump every few years, depending on where rates are when the fix ends.

## What each system teaches

- **Taiwan:** low rates are not guaranteed. Stress-test your payment at 1 percentage point higher.
- **US:** long fixed rates give certainty, but the initial rate matters enormously over 30 years.
- **UK:** always know when your deal ends, and plan the next one months in advance.

Whichever system you are in, the same habits help: keep an emergency fund, keep your payment comfortably within your income, and run the numbers before you sign. Try all three modes in our [mortgage calculator](/mortgage).
