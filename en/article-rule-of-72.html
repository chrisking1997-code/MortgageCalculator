2026 Financial Must-Learn: How Many Years to Double Your Assets? The "Rule of 72" Used by the Rich

Published: April 2026

"When exactly will my money double?" This is the most frequently asked question by everyone entering the investment market. Instead of relying on feelings or blindly trusting stock tips, it's better to learn the financial industry's most classic quick-calculation formula—the "Rule of 72." Once you master this logic, you can see through the cruelty of inflation and the terrifying power of compound interest in a single second.

I. What is the Rule of 72?

The Rule of 72 is a mathematical formula used to quickly calculate the "time required to double the principal."
The formula is very simple: 72 ÷ Annualized Rate of Return = Number of years required to double the principal.
You don't need a complex scientific calculator; you can get the answer directly in your head. The core of this formula is based on the compounding effect, which Einstein called the "eighth wonder of the world."

II. Cruel Comparison: Fixed Deposits vs. Market ETFs

Let's use the Rule of 72 to look at the real power of different financial tools:

  • Bank Fixed Deposit (Assuming 2% annual interest rate): 72 ÷ 2 = 36 years. This means if you put 1 million in the bank, you have to wait exactly 36 years for it to become 2 million. In the high-inflation environment of 2026, the purchasing power of that 2 million after 36 years will have long been eaten away by inflation.
  • Steady Market ETF (Assuming 7% annualized return): 72 ÷ 7 ≈ 10.2 years. The same 1 million, just placed in a market-cap ETF that tracks economic growth, can double to 2 million in about 10 years. If you have an investment horizon of 30 years, your assets will experience a geometric explosion of "doubling 3 times" (1 million ➡️ 2 million ➡️ 4 million ➡️ 8 million).
